## v4 strategic update (2026-04-22, one-sentence bottom line)

> **OGeez is a filmmaker-led commercial studio with festival credibility and a bilingual bench. The three adjectives — *filmmaker-led*, *festival-credentialed*, *bilingual* — are the unmovable positioning claims. Every craft decision, on-camera choice, deck structure, hire, and pitch either defends one of those three or erodes the position.**

This sentence overrides nothing in v3. It clarifies the *why* behind everything in v3:

- **Why "loud, vibrant, expressive"**: because filmmaker-led work signs its choices visibly. The frame can carry what the copy withholds. This is the signal that the director is in the room.
- **Why "a changing voice"**: because festival-credentialed directors don't have one register — they have the right one for the material. The shift is the skill.
- **Why bilingual first-class**: because the regional slot — the one Academy Films or Somesuch would sit in if they were from Cairo — requires native bilingual craft. This is the house's moat.
- **Why the 10+1 craft moves + 3 tonal registers (DNA synthesis §2, §9)**: because a filmmaker-led studio is defined by a repertoire, not a signature. The repertoire is the proof that the directing is real.
- **Why canon-silent (INSTRUCTIONS §2.4, validated as competitive signal in DNA §15.5)**: because festival-credentialed work doesn't need to name-check the canon — the influences are baked into the craft choices. Naming them back gives the pitch away as a crutch.

**Applied to pitches**: when writing a deck, ask "does this defend filmmaker-led, festival-credentialed, or bilingual?" If a line doesn't defend one of the three, cut it or rewrite it.

**Applied to hires**: every hire — director, strategist, producer, post lead — is evaluated on whether they strengthen one of the three claims. BD hires that don't strengthen the position are deferred.

**Applied to clients**: the book prioritizes clients who pay for the three claims. Mid-tier content-reel volume work that doesn't pay for craft gets declined.

## Executive Summary

OGeez Studios is a Cairo-based production house, creative agency, and film studio — all three, on purpose. The Arabic is أوچييز ستوديوز (the چ preserves the hard-G), the legal mark is OGZ Studios, the spoken name is OGeez. Cairo handles the creative (Ahmed Kamel, Amira Salah). Riyadh handles the commercial (Alhareth Albelaihed, Managing Partner).

The house voice is anchored in one sentence from the brand manual's own logo concept: **loud, vibrant, expressive**. The promise one page earlier anchors the second half: **a changing voice**. Those two lines together are the brand's operating system. The studio's identity is *supposed* to shape-shift scene to scene and client to client — which is why the logo has twelve moods and why the manual reads as both corporate-identity document and punk zine. The tension is the product.

Content in this voice is bilingual first-class (Egyptian dialect for Egypt, Saudi dialect for KSA, MSA only when asked), refuses gloss, refuses aggression, and keeps a straight face while being funny. Where a competitor studio reaches for a word, OGeez reaches for an image; where a competitor would declare, OGeez would undercut.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|---|---|
| **Loud, vibrant, expressive** — we build for **attention**, not **volume**. Images that earn the room, not shout at it. | **Aggressive / shouting** — we don't push the viewer. "Our cuts are too quiet" = the audience got a second to think. We build for that second. |
| **A changing voice** — twelve logo moods, Egyptian/Saudi/MSA fluency, we shift register by scene and client. | **Gloss** — no polish for polish's sake. The seams show on purpose. |
| **Funny with a straight face** — the joke is in the framing, not the punchline. | **Punchline-chasing** — we don't set up and knock down. We let the image do it. |
| **Bilingual first-class** — Arabic and English live at the same level. Neither is a translation of the other. | **MSA-by-default** — MSA only when a client asks for it. Dialect is the default. |
| **Corporate precision + punk zine** — the spec sheet and the fanzine at the same time. The contradiction is the identity. | **Generic agency** — no "innovative solutions for forward-thinking brands." |
| **Arabic, funny, and beautiful as the starting point** — not a compromise, not a value-add. | **Borrowed gestures** — we don't ape a Western studio, a Gulf studio, or a Cairo elder studio. |

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### 1. Loud, vibrant, expressive
- **What it means**: Saturated color choices, confident typography, images that stand at full height. Energy is in the frame, not the copy. Copy can be quiet; the frame is always loud.
- **How it shows up**: Primary palette (Electric Green + Electric Ultramarine + Epink) appears at full strength, never as tint. Headlines set big, never timid. Reel cuts land hard and hold. Silence is allowed — but only as a loud choice.
- **What to avoid**: Pastels, 60% tints, cautious typography, hedged statements, "soft launch" visual language.
- **Evidence**: Brand manual §2.01 — "A cutting edge new voice in the filmmaking industry that is loud, vibrant and expressive." Logo uses neon lime on magenta drop-shadow as the default lockup.
- **Confidence**: High

#### 2. A changing voice
- **What it means**: The brand is *supposed* to shift. The logo has twelve mood variants (Action!, Standby, It's A Wrap, Zzzz, IWD, Halloween, and others) because the voice shifts by context, not because the brand is inconsistent. Consistency is in the *shape of the shift*, not in a fixed tone.
- **How it shows up**: Egyptian dialect for Egyptian clients and audiences. Saudi dialect (Najdi/Hijazi/Khaleeji-neutral per the dialect guide) for KSA. English that's confident enough to drop into Arabic mid-sentence without apology. Client-specific overrides are welcome — see Tone-by-Context.
- **What to avoid**: Treating "changing voice" as a license to be inconsistent within a single piece. One piece, one register — the change happens *across* pieces.
- **Evidence**: Brand manual §1.03 Brand Promise — "A changing voice." Manual mood-logo system. Ahmed, 2026-04-21: corporate/punk split is intentional; Zain's voice is a client override, not a house rule.
- **Confidence**: High

#### 3. Funny with a straight face
- **What it means**: Comedy-first, but the comedy is in the framing, the juxtaposition, the withheld moment — not in a gag or punchline. A straight face that makes you laugh. وش جاد بيضحّك.
- **How it shows up**: Deadpan captions next to absurd images. A corporate-looking stationery system printed in neon lime. A mascot mouth holding an airplane. Bureaucracy treated as visual comedy rather than complaint.
- **What to avoid**: Setup/punchline structures. Jokes that explain themselves. Parody of the thing we're promoting. Meme-format humor that ages in a week.
- **Evidence**: Tagline pick — "A straight face that makes you laugh." Project Mabeen tonal register in INSTRUCTIONS.md ("comedy-first; the bureaucracy is the joke and the horror at the same time"). Logo's mouth-mascot and twelve moods.
- **Confidence**: High

#### 4. Bilingual first-class
- **What it means**: Arabic and English are both primary. Neither translates the other. A headline can live in either language, and the companion language is a sibling, not a subtitle.
- **How it shows up**: Modern Era (familytype.co) for English, Neue Frutiger Arabic (Nadine Chahine) for Arabic — paired at equal optical weight. Dialect choice is the client's dialect; Egyptian if Egyptian, Saudi if Saudi, MSA only if the brief demands. The OGeez name itself is trilingual: OGZ / OGeez / أوچييز.
- **What to avoid**: Treating English as "the real version" and Arabic as translation. Using MSA as a neutral default when dialect would hit harder. Arabic set smaller than English. Google-translate rhythms in either language.
- **Evidence**: Brand manual typography pairings. Instagram handle @ogz_studios + page title "OGz Studios - أوچييز ستوديوز". Saudi dialect guide (9,700 words, house-level asset). Ahmed's personal working habit (Arabic + English first-class, per INSTRUCTIONS.md).
- **Confidence**: High

#### 5. Corporate precision + punk zine
- **What it means**: The brand's internal tension is the brand. A 282-page corporate identity manual, designed like a 90s fanzine. Serious typography, absurd mood names. The precision is real; the attitude is real; neither softens the other.
- **How it shows up**: A rate card can exist. It should still feel a little bit illegal. Client deliverables are tight and spec-compliant; the accompanying WhatsApp message can be one word. The invoice template has a joke in the footer.
- **What to avoid**: Picking one side of the tension. If you feel corporate, add zine. If you feel zine, add rigor.
- **Evidence**: Ahmed, 2026-04-21 ("mix of both"). 46-page condensed manual in neon lime cover; manual's contents include stationery spec + mood-logo variants side by side.
- **Confidence**: High

## Native craft moves (from shipped work)

Patterns that appear across the founder note, the EWCF third-pass, and the Zain 5G cultural brief. These are the house *moves* — the structural verbs of OGeez copy, independent of subject matter.

### 1. Inversion
Not "X," but *the inverse of X*. The brief's framing is flipped and the inversion becomes the idea.
- *"We are a commentary layer with a league under it — inversion, not imitation."* (EWCF)
- *"We don't make a campaign about gamers. We make the campaign that gamers are talking over."* (EWCF)
- *"MENA is not the audience travelling to a tournament — the tournament is the guest arriving to the region."* (EWCF)
- *"Work that earns attention instead of demanding it."* (Founder note)
- *"5G is amplification, not replacement."* (Zain brief)

Rule: if the client's brief says "we want X," the OGeez response usually starts by naming the inverse of X and shipping *that*.

### 2. Refusal framing
Define by what we don't do. Mission C lives here. So does the founder note's "We don't love being called a 'content partner.'"
- *"We don't do aggressive. We don't do gloss."* (Tagline/Mission)
- *"No trailer voice. No music under the dialogue."* (EWCF hero register)
- *"No slogan is required."* (EWCF Saudi-first)
- *"Not with a flag. With a seating order."* (EWCF)

Rule: every positioning sentence should be pressure-testable as a refusal. If it could be said by any studio, cut it.

### 3. Specific-over-impressive
"We'd rather be specific than impressive" is a stated value in the founder note — and it shows up in every shipped piece. Concrete nouns over abstract adjectives. *A booth. A bench. A production calendar.* Not *a platform. An ecosystem. An engine.* (Unless "engine" names a specific production object.)
- *"A booth, a bench, and a production calendar."* (EWCF)
- *"A container in Riyadh, one in Cairo, one virtual pod."* (EWCF)
- *"The wall is full of words before it's full of light."* (EWCF)
- *"A composed frame, a real performance, a reason for every cut."* (Founder note)

Rule: adjective + noun should lose to noun + noun. *"Innovative campaign"* → *"a commentary layer."*

### 4. Attention vs. volume
The house distinction for anything "loud." Volume *demands* attention; the work *earns* it. This is the preferred phrasing over "loud vs. aggressive" — Ahmed uses it natively.
- *"We like clients who know the difference between attention and volume."* (Founder note)
- *"Work that earns attention instead of demanding it."* (Founder note)
- *"We've had clients tell us our cuts are 'too quiet.' That usually means the audience got a second to think. We like that second."* (Founder note)

Rule: copy can be quiet. The frame can be loud. Collapsing the two is the failure mode.

### 5. Probability-weighted counterpoints (INSTRUCTIONS §2.7)
In strategic docs, counterpoints arrive with a numeric weight. This is the studio's native way of disagreeing with itself on paper.
- *"P ≈ 0.45. Mitigation: [...]"* (EWCF counterpoints section)
- Applies to pitch decks, treatments, and strategy docs. Not to captions.

### 6. Dialect as craft, not cosmetic
Dialect choice is a production decision, same class as a lens choice. Never treated as cast-and-costume.
- *"Dialect is craft, not cosmetic."* (Founder note)
- *"Egyptian for Egypt, Saudi for Saudi, Gulf for the Gulf."* (Founder note)
- *"The booth argues in dialect. Where there's no story, the booth makes one."* (EWCF)
- Rule: "make it local" is heard as a demand on the writing, not the voice-cast.

### 7. The second of silence
A recurring structural choice — a beat of silence or a withheld image where a competitor would fill with music or punchline. "The second to think" is the unit of OGeez craft.
- *"At 0:58 the mics cut. One beat of silence. End card."* (EWCF hero)
- *"Silence is allowed — but only as a loud choice."* (v1 voice attribute)
- **Corpus corroboration:** Roshn Hope Core monologues end on a held beat (`previous-work/roshn-month4.md`). NHC CEO Cityscape is paced around TED pauses (`nhc-cityscape-ceo.md`). Ebny match-cuts land on silence before dialogue returns (`ebny.md`).

Rule: if the piece is three seconds too long, the second to cut is almost never the quiet one.

### 8. Pull-back reveal (v3, from corpus — the Bank Aljazira formula)
Start tight on a specific object, pull back to reveal the scale/context, and **the reveal is the punchline**. The camera move is the storytelling.
- `previous-work/aljazira-bank.md` — all six reels in the Bank Aljazira PPM use this formula. Corpus-defining.
- `previous-work/sign-icecream.md` concept 3 — camera pans across crowd, pulls back to reveal ice cream.
- `previous-work/flavor-homeland.md` — each hand holding the note pulls back to show whose hand it is.
- `previous-work/zaka.md` concept A — falcon POV pulls back across historical eras.

Rule: the house uses pull-back where a less disciplined studio would use a cut. Give the viewer the object first and the meaning second.

**Probability-weighted counterpoint** (*P ≈ 0.35*): eight+ uses across the corpus. Becoming a tell. **Mitigation:** rotate the reveal-object type — don't let it always be the product/logo. Use for character reveal, location reveal, or punchline reveal.

### 9. Scale-concrete comparison (v3, from corpus — the NHC 10M formula)
"*This is 1 X. This is not even 10M X.*" The move reframes a scale claim by showing how much the viewer's intuition *under*estimates the scale.
- `previous-work/nhc-10m.md` — *"هذي خوذة وحدة / وهذي؟ عشرين خوذة…"* — named-first corpus example; one helmet / twenty helmets / still not 10M helmets, repeated with nails and hours.
- `previous-work/nhc-natday.md` — the "Saudi heritage" claim is made via a concrete scene list (market, sewing, musician, horseman, family), not via montage of feelings.
- `previous-work/batates-zalabya.md` — 3 pillars × 3 reels = 9 reels, as a scale-concrete content-plan statement.

Rule: when a brief contains a big number, don't say the number. Show 1 unit, show 20 units, show how far that is from the big number.

**Probability-weighted counterpoint** (*P ≈ 0.25*): the move has a ceiling. Only works when the big number is genuinely hard to picture. "We sold 50 cars" should not use this move.

### 10. POV-as-narrator (v3, from corpus — the falcon / banknote / helmet move)
The camera *is* a character. A non-human or inanimate object holds the POV and the entire story is told from its perspective. **Silent object-POV** — the object tracks motion but doesn't speak.
- `previous-work/zaka.md` concept A — falcon POV tracking Saudi history.
- `previous-work/flavor-homeland.md` — 24 SAR banknote POV through 8 hands.
- `previous-work/nhc-10m.md` — helmet / vest / belt POV.
- `previous-work/drinkle.md` — bottle POV in parts of the hero film.

Rule: POV-as-narrator replaces voiceover. The viewer is inside the object. The object must stay silent — talking objects collapse the move.

**Probability-weighted counterpoint** (*P ≈ 0.40*): object-POV becomes gimmicky the moment the object speaks. The corpus examples all use silent object-POV; that discipline is load-bearing.

### 11. Match-cut past/present (v3, from corpus — the Ebny / Flavor of Homeland / Zaka A move)
Two shots, different eras, identical framing and motion — the cut collapses time.
- `previous-work/ebny.md` — three siblings, past and present, match-cut at specific identical motions.
- `previous-work/flavor-homeland.md` — Sign cashier's hand in present = farmer's hand decades earlier, same motion.
- `previous-work/zaka.md` concept A — falcon-POV match-cuts across eras. Concept B — dagger-in-present match-cuts to dagger-in-historical-scenes.

Rule: match-cut past/present replaces narration for any "heritage meets modern" brief. Let the cut do the work.

## Tonal registers (v3, from corpus)

Beyond the 11 numbered moves, three **tonal registers** recur often enough to deserve their own names. A register is a bigger-than-move stylistic envelope that shapes multiple craft decisions at once.

### Register A — Netflix-mockumentary
Talking-head interviews, subtitled aside-to-camera, chyron lower-thirds played for irony.
- `previous-work/aljazeera-capital.md` — comic mockumentary on a coffee machine.
- `previous-work/pure-group.md` — earnest mockumentary on a 2015 setback.
- `previous-work/roshn-month4.md` — mockumentary grammar applied to scripted reels (cast-to-camera existential asides).
- `previous-work/daw-shopping.md` — "Sultan" archetype-coach as talking-head over archetype reels.

Rule: use when the subject is *people*, not products. Register humanizes a brand by humanizing its subjects.

**Probability-weighted counterpoint** (*P ≈ 0.30*): register peaked culturally 2017-2022. May read retro now; stale in three years. **Mitigation:** fuse with Register B or C for hybrids that don't sit cleanly in the mockumentary box.

### Register B — Thought-leader speech
Composed, paced, TED-grammar with collage stage visuals. Arabic literary register (not MSA-bureaucratic, not dialect).
- `previous-work/nhc-cityscape-ceo.md` — the single purest corpus example. *"نبني للإنسان."*

Rule: reserved for CEO / founder / state-actor briefs. House skill: writing Arabic literary register that carries a ~12-minute speech without losing the room.

### Register C — Vertical comedy (هلس)
Short-form sketch, 2-3 minutes, written for vertical video. Single location, two leads, escalating absurd premise.
- `previous-work/ya-layla-beida.md` — Karim Fekri, 35-50 eps × 2-3 min; six genre tags *كوميدي / سكتشي / هلس / سريع / صاخب*; 70% single apartment.

Rule: the "هلس" tag is a real house aesthetic — **Egyptian absurdist vernacular comedy**, distinct from the "funny with a straight face" house default. Don't collapse the two.

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Key Principle |

| Recruiting / internal | Low | High | Low | Punk zine full strength. No HR-speak. |

### v3 corpus refinements (three clarifications)

#### On-camera palette ≠ house palette
The **house palette** (OGZ Electric Green + Electric Ultramarine + Epink, per Chubby Studio manual) lives in *deck wrapping* — decks, stationery, identity. The **on-camera palette** is **client-tier-deterministic**, not house-deterministic. The corpus shows three on-camera modes:
- **Corporate / institutional** (restrained, muted, black + accent): Bank Aljazira, NHC CEO Cityscape, Pure Group, Al Jazeera Capital
- **Consumer / pop** (saturation-up, pattern-forward): Drinkle, Sign Ice Cream, Roshn Hope Core, Daw Shopping
- **Heritage / patriotic** (earth tones, art-direction-heavy): NHC National Day, Zaka, Flavor of Homeland

v2 implied the Electric-Green palette is the studio's on-camera signature. v3 corrects: it's the *voice wrapping*, not the *work wrapping*.

#### The OGZ proposal template is itself a craft move
A standing deck structure recurs across the corpus (cleanest in `batates-zalabya.md` and `habbar.md`):

> About OGZ → Clients → Agencies & Partners → Project Overview → Objective → Content Plan → Brand Essence → Creative Tone & Aesthetic → Direction → Pillars (usually 3×3 grid) → Photos close-out

The rhythm is the move. Sections can be skipped / combined / reordered; the rhythm stays. This template signals the studio's discipline before any idea lands.

**Probability-weighted counterpoint** (*P ≈ 0.30*): senior creatives on the client side may read the rhythm as "agency slide deck." **Mitigation:** for repeat or sophisticated clients, compress sections 1-3 to a 3-line deck-footer signature, not a 4-slide front matter.

#### Adjacent brands — Cairolog and الشلة الأصلية
The corpus surfaces two brands credited alongside OGZ:
- **Cairolog** — founded by Mohamed Rabie (Warsaw Film School). The Sign *Flavor of Homeland* film (`flavor-homeland.md`) is credited *"by Cairolog / Director Ahmed Kamel"*. Relationship unresolved — sister, predecessor, or adjacent entity. See open question #11 below.
- **الشلة الأصلية للإنتاج الفني** — OGZ's shorts arm. 6 shorts produced; one won 3 European awards 2025 (Riyadh / Warsaw / Cairo). Shares personnel with OGZ (Amira Salah Managing Partner / Co-Producer; Ahmed Kamel Co-Producer; Ahmed Zayan Head of Production). Brand attribution question — are the shorts "OGeez presents" or adjacent-house? See open question #12.

Neither brand is absorbed into the OGeez voice. They use the same personnel but carry their own credits. Client-facing copy should not collapse them into "OGeez," and v3 treats them as documented-adjacent.

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Instagram / TikTok caption
- **Overall tone**: Deadpan, bilingual, short.
- **Opening approach**: Image or idea first. Never "Excited to share…"
- **Do's**: Mix EN and AR freely. Use one emoji max, or none. Keep under 15 words.
- **Don'ts**: Corporate hashtags. "Swipe to see." Emoji soup. Translation of the English into Arabic (or vice versa) — sibling copy, not mirror copy.
- **Example (Egyptian)**: *الفيلم اتصوّر في شارع واحد. ثلاث أيام. الشارع ما لاحظش.*
- **Example (English)**: *Shot in one street. Three days. The street didn't notice.*

#### Client-facing email (new client)
- **Overall tone**: Plain, warm, confident.
- **Opening approach**: Subject line is a sentence, not a category. No "Quick question" or "Following up."
- **Do's**: Greet, land the ask in sentence two, end with a concrete next step.
- **Don'ts**: "I hope this finds you well." Long preamble. Emoji.
- **Example subject**: *Cairo shoot, Tues–Thurs — here's the call sheet I'd need approved.*

#### Pitch deck / treatment
- **Overall tone**: Image-first, then craft reasoning.
- **Opening approach**: Open on a moodboard image or a single-sentence logline. Never on "About OGeez."
- **Do's**: Keep craft decisions (blocking, image logic, cut causality) explicit. One reference per idea, maximum.
- **Don'ts**: Name-drop directors, films, or studios in the deck — absorb them as craft choices. "This will be the Arabic [X]" is banned.
- **Example opener**: *Scene one: a clerk waits for his own name to be called. He has been waiting since before the office opened.*

#### Saudi-market work (general, non-Zain)
- **Mandatory cross-references for any KSA brief:**
  - `agency/templates/saudi-creative-references.md` — standing KSA creative reference (15 award-winning Saudi campaigns read for the craft move; pop-culture inventory; derivation method using the four-pattern checklist: *retrieve-don't-invent, refuse pan-Arab dilution, cast real people lightly-directed, reframe the category pain*).
  - `agency/templates/saudi-dialect-tone-guide.md` — full register ladder. House rule: default to **Khaleeji-neutral** unless the brand position calls for Najdi warmth or Hijazi ease.
- The KSA creative-references file is loaded automatically by `brand-voice:enforce-voice` when the client country is KSA (see *Notes on enforcement* at the end of this document).

#### Zain KSA (client override)
- **Override active**: the Zain mandate is "funny + beautiful, never aggressive." House "loud" is dialled one notch down. Still shape-shifting within the Zain brand system.
- **Do's**: Gentle comedy. Prioritize beauty of the frame over punch of the idea.
- **Don'ts**: Edge humor. Irony at the brand's expense. Loud palette unless co-branded pieces allow it.

#### Festival submission (Mabeen, Timo, Halayeb, shorts)
- **Filmmaker voice, not agency voice.** No brand moods, no neon, no zine seams. Selection committees read dry craft copy and switch off on flash.
- **Do's**: Describe the film in the film's own register. Tone-down humor. Let the logline do the work.
- **Don'ts**: "A bold new voice in MENA cinema." Agency color. Zine formatting.

## Language That Works

### Top phrases (sampled from shipped work)

1. **"A changing voice."** / **صوت متغيّر.** — Brand promise. All-purpose header, section break, sign-off.
2. **"A straight face that makes you laugh."** / **وش جاد بيضحّك.** — Tagline. Works for dry decks, captions that don't have an image yet.
3. **"We don't do aggressive. We don't do gloss."** — Mission. Position statement for pitches where the client needs to hear what they won't get.
4. **"We like clients who know the difference between attention and volume."** — Founder-note line. Best qualifier sentence in the house repertoire; reuse-able in "who we work with" copy.
5. **"Work that earns attention instead of demanding it."** — Founder note. Short-form positioning that carries the entire attention/volume distinction without naming it.
6. **"We'd rather be specific than impressive."** — Founder note. One-line counter to every agency-speak pitch you'll ever write against.
7. **"Dialect is craft, not cosmetic."** — Founder note. Definitive sentence on the bilingual-first-class principle.
8. **"Not with a flag. With a seating order."** — EWCF. The house way of describing Saudi-first / regional-priority positioning without tokenism. Reusable template: *Not with [declaration]. With [structural choice].*
9. **"[X], not [inverse of X]."** — EWCF inversion template. *"Inversion, not imitation."* *"Amplification, not replacement."* *"A production methodology, not a metaphor."* Use anywhere the client brief reaches for the obvious framing — flip it.
10. **"Saatchi and Leo Burnett will arrive with metaphors. We arrive with [specific object]."** — EWCF pitch move. Template for pitch rooms where the competitive set is named studios. Replace [specific object] with the actual production artefact the pitch hinges on.
11. **"If it weren't uncomfortable, it wouldn't be differentiating."** — EWCF. The house defence of the uncomfortable creative choice (INSTRUCTIONS §2 rule 10 made load-bearing in copy).
12. **"For the first time, the talking comes from us."** / **لأول مرة، الكلام مننا.** — EWCF platform line. Archetype of the OGeez opening move: compressed, inverted, dialect-native, bilingual sibling.

### Questions that engage (for pitches, discovery calls)

1. **"What's the image we're building toward?"** — Moves the brief away from deliverables and toward craft.
2. **"If you shut off the audio, does the piece still work?"** — Elicits the visual idea.
3. **"Which part of this is supposed to be beautiful, and which part is supposed to be funny?"** — Straight-face / joke separation.
4. **"هو الإعلان بيتكلّم عربي بأي لهجة؟"** — Surfaces the dialect choice early.
5. **"Is the brief asking for attention, or asking for volume?"** — Opens the house distinction at kickoff instead of at revision. Best used in the first 20 minutes of discovery.

### Opening-move templates (sampled)

- *"Diagnosis (one sentence):"* — EWCF pattern. Every strategic doc opens with a single-sentence problem statement before anything else. Locks the frame.
- *"What the research actually changed:"* — EWCF pattern. Surfaces what's new *since last round*. Signals that we are reading, not declaring.
- *"Three structural strategies (per INSTRUCTIONS §2):"* — Every time we offer alternatives. Exactly three, labeled by structure.
- *"Single most important fix:"* — EWCF pattern. Closes every strategic doc with the one thing that changes downstream.
- *"Counter-points (probability-weighted):"* — Standing rule visible in copy. `P ≈ 0.XX. Mitigation: [...]`

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns

1. **Declarative mission statements.** — Problem: fights the brand's own "changing voice" promise and Ahmed's picked list-of-no's mission. Better: positioning by refusal or by image.
2. **Naming canon references in client-facing work.** — Problem: collapses the craft into a label ("A24-style," "the Arabic [X]"). Better: execute the craft choice (blocking, cut causality, withheld moment) and let it land unnamed.
3. **Mirror-translating English into Arabic.** — Problem: kills the bilingual-first-class principle; makes Arabic feel like a subtitle. Better: sibling copy — same meaning, different sentence shapes, different rhythms.
4. **Emoji soup in captions.** — Problem: undercuts the straight face. Better: one emoji maximum, or none. Never more than one per caption.
5. **"Excited to announce…"** — Problem: dead opener, inherited from agency LinkedIn. Better: open on the image, the line, or the line break. *Shot. Three days. One street.*
6. **"Innovative / cutting-edge / disruptive."** — Problem: even the brand manual uses "cutting edge" once; this guideline overrides that. Better: the proof is in the cut, the image, and the frame — not in the adjective.
7. **Glossing the tension between "loud" and "not aggressive."** — Problem: *aggressive* = pushes the viewer. *Loud* = occupies the room. Copy that conflates them drifts into YELL-CASE and shout-lines. Better: loud in the frame, quiet in the copy. Let the image shout; the sentence can whisper.

## Content Examples (sampled from shipped work)

### Excellent — Founder-note paragraph (long-form positioning)

> I started OGeez with Amira because we were tired of two things at the same time. Work that took itself seriously and was still boring. And work that was funny but embarrassed to be anything more than funny. The region does both of those on loop, and we wanted a place where neither one was the default.

**Source**: `agency/ogeez/identity-2026-04-21.md` — founder note opening.
**Why it's on-brand**: Refusal framing (*tired of two things*). Inversion (two opposites, both refused, OGeez is the space neither occupies). Specific-over-impressive (*boring*, *embarrassed* — concrete, not abstract). Opens on personal reason, not on claims about the studio. Zero adjectives doing the heavy lifting.

### Excellent — Pitch-deck diagnosis (one-sentence problem statement)

> For the first time in esports history, MENA gamers are hosts, not guests — and the only pre-event work that will land is the one that gives them the microphone instead of pointing one at them.

**Source**: `agency/ewcf/pre-event-campaign-third-pass.md` §1.
**Why it's on-brand**: Inversion in both halves (hosts/guests, give the mic / point it). Compressed to 35 words carrying a thesis + a platform + a refusal. Opens a strategic doc by locking the frame before any ideas land. Reusable template: *"For the first time in [X], [audience] is [inverted role] — and the only [work type] that will land is the one that [refusal/inversion]."*

### Excellent — Platform line (bilingual sibling, not translation)

> **كومنتاري. لأول مرة، الكلام مننا.**
> *Commentary. For the first time, the talking comes from us.*

**Source**: `agency/ewcf/pre-event-campaign-third-pass.md` §4.
**Why it's on-brand**: Arabic first. The English isn't a translation — *"the talking comes from us"* carries a different register than *الكلام مننا*, and the choice is deliberate. Opens with a loan-word (*كومنتاري*) to signal the campaign is in the scene's own vernacular. Compressed. Direct. Dialect as craft.

### Excellent — Qualifying sentence (who we work with)

> We like clients who know the difference between attention and volume.

**Source**: Founder note.
**Why it's on-brand**: One line that does the job of a 200-word qualification slide. Names the house distinction. Filters clients by whether they already understand the distinction — no explanation required. Reusable as an email closer, a capabilities-deck sign-off, a contract preamble.

### Excellent — Pitch-room counter-positioning

> Saatchi and Leo Burnett will arrive with metaphors. We arrive with a booth, a bench, and a production calendar.

**Source**: EWCF third-pass §8.
**Why it's on-brand**: Specific-over-impressive as a literal sentence structure — *metaphors* (abstract) versus *booth, bench, production calendar* (three concrete nouns). Names the competitive set without insulting them. Positions the studio on production capacity, not on creative credentials. Template: *"[Competitor tier] will arrive with [abstract noun]. We arrive with [three specific objects]."*

### Excellent — Caption-style inversion

> We don't make a campaign about gamers. We make the campaign that gamers are talking over.

**Source**: EWCF third-pass §4.
**Why it's on-brand**: The defining house move (inversion) compressed to two sentences. Refusal + inversion in one beat. Could be an Instagram caption unchanged. The inversion itself is the idea.

### Off-brand — what to catch and fix

**Original:**
> Excited to announce our latest innovative campaign for [Client] — a cutting-edge storytelling experience that redefines what's possible in MENA content. 🚀🎬✨

**What's wrong**: "Excited to announce" (dead opener). "Innovative," "cutting-edge," "storytelling" (three banned words in one sentence). "MENA content" (two banned terms — MENA + content). Emoji soup. Agency register. No image. Describes feelings nobody has ("excited") — directly violates the founder note's *"We don't do decks that describe feelings nobody actually has."*

**Fix, in the house voice:**
> New work with [Client]. Not a campaign about [category]. The campaign that [category] is talking over.
> شغل جديد مع [الكلاينت]. مش حملة عن [الفئة]. الحملة اللي [الفئة] بتتكلم فوقها.

**Why the fix is on-brand**: Inversion (the EWCF move, reapplied). Specific-over-impressive (*new work* not *innovative campaign*). Arabic as sibling, not translation (notice the Arabic shortens naturally; the English is slightly longer — that's correct, not a bug). Zero adjectives carrying weight. No emoji.

## Notes on enforcement

These guidelines are designed to be loaded by `/brand-voice:enforce-voice`. Three standing rules apply on top of everything in this document. The first two are from Ahmed's personal operating system (INSTRUCTIONS.md §2); the third is the KSA auto-load rule added 2026-04-22.

- **Absorb canon references. Never name them.** The craft lands in the blocking, the cut, the image, the withheld moment. It never lands in the copy as a reference.
- **When offering alternatives, give exactly three, labeled by structural strategy, never by tone or risk.** Applies when the enforcement skill generates options for a headline, a tagline, a caption, or a scene line.
- **When `client.country = KSA`, also load `agency/templates/saudi-creative-references.md` and `agency/templates/saudi-dialect-tone-guide.md` alongside this document.** The creative-references file is the KSA-specific campaign + pop-culture + derivation reference; the tone guide is the dialect playbook. Both are mandatory, not optional, for any Saudi-market output — captions, scripts, decks, emails. The four-pattern checklist in the creative-references file (*retrieve-don't-invent, refuse pan-Arab dilution, cast real people lightly-directed, reframe category pain*) is the quality bar: generated KSA concepts should hit at least three of four before being presented.

### v4.1 Two-track proposal structure

From 2026-05-01 forward, the enforcement skill should select proposal structure based on `client.status`:

- **`client.status = first_time`**: use the 11-section OGZ proposal template rhythm (DNA synthesis §3), but compress sections 1-3 (*About OGZ / Clients / Agencies & Partners*) into a 3-line deck *footer*, not 4 front-matter slides. Front-matter stays idea-first from slide 1.
- **`client.status = repeat`** (ROSHN, NHC, Sign, Bank Aljazira, Almarai, Shawarma House, and any future client at month 3+ of a standing engagement): **idea-first structure.** Slide 1 = the idea (one line + one image). Slide 2 = the craft spine (named moves from DNA §2). Slide 3 = deliverables matrix. Credentials are a 3-line signature at close, not an opening.

**Why**: the 11-section rhythm is legible as "agency deck" to senior creatives (DNA §10 C5). Repeat clients don't need the re-pitch; first-time clients do. One house, two postures, same template library.

**Test bed**: the Zain Shabab Revamp pitch (launch 2026-05-02) gets idea-first; Zen Clinic gets standard. Compare reception.

### v4.3 The positioning-defense test for every line of copy

When the enforcement skill generates or validates a sentence destined for a client-facing deck, caption, or script, it should run a 3-question test:

1. **Does this sentence defend *filmmaker-led*?** — i.e., does it implicitly or explicitly credit the director, show directorial thinking, or name a craft choice?
2. **Does this sentence defend *festival-credentialed*?** — i.e., does it carry the tonal discipline of festival-grade work (restraint, specificity, withheld moment)?
3. **Does this sentence defend *bilingual*?** — i.e., does it either live natively in one dialect or, if paired with a translation, treat the two as siblings not subtitles?

A line doesn't need to hit all three. It should hit at least one. Lines that hit zero either get cut or rewritten.

### v4.5 Positioning hierarchy (for all client-facing copy)

When writing OGeez's own copy (about-us, website, bio, credentials footer):

1. **Filmmaker-led commercial studio** — the headline noun.
2. **With festival credibility** — the qualifier that lifts commercial to studio.
3. **Bilingual** — the regional differentiator.
4. **Three cities at maturity (Cairo + Riyadh + one more by 2028)** — the operational footprint.
5. **A documented craft method** — the 10+1 moves, the 3 registers, the brief-type matrix. Mentioned by existence, not in full.

Everything else (client book, director roster, adjacent practices) is *evidence*, not headline.

### v4.6 What NOT to say about OGeez in 2026

These phrases are prohibited in client-facing copy (in-house working language is fine):
