# Positioning — Attention, not volume.

## The line

**Attention, not volume.**

Four words. Ships alongside the wordmark; the studio name lives elsewhere on the page. Assumes the reader is already in the room — a self-selecting filter for clients, hires, and peers.

## What it does

Replaces v4's positioning sentence (*filmmaker-led commercial studio with festival credibility and a bilingual bench*) with a sharper, shorter line that:

- Defends the *attention vs volume* craft distinction (manifesto ¶4)
- Reads at the bar — short, declarative, no agency padding
- Carries the v5 manifesto's compressed truth without naming it

## Where it appears

- Email signature (under the wordmark)
- Deck cover footer (small, right-aligned)
- Hire ad headline (or sub-line)
- Website footer
- Recruiting one-pagers
- Festival submission cover (alongside *OGeez Presents*)

## Where it does NOT appear

- As an opener line in the manifesto (the manifesto opens with *في مستوى.*)
- In place of the manifesto in any internal document
- Standalone without the wordmark, in client-facing work
- As a tagline overlay on the mouth (the mouth is a discipline mark, not a billboard)

## Bilingual sibling (when both languages appear together)

The line stays English-default. When Arabic is needed alongside, the sibling is:

**الانتباه، مش الحجم.**

(Egyptian dialect, idea-native — preserves the manifesto's voice. Use the diamond ◆ as the divider when both appear in the same surface.)

## How to defend the line

If a stakeholder asks "what does *Attention, not volume* mean?" — point at manifesto ¶4:

> *We build for attention, not volume. A loud frame is not a loud cut. A loud cut is not a loud line. We let the image carry what the copy withholds. The second the audience gets to think is the second the work was built around.*

The four-word line is the compressed proof. The paragraph is the full claim. Never ship one without the other available.
